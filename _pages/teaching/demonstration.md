I presented a short lesson on pedagogical content knowledge (PCK) to peers and an instructor, focusing on how subject knowledge and teaching strategies come together in instruction. The session included opportunities for questions and participation.

## Instructor feedback

The instructor's written feedback addressed the lesson's goals, organization, content, teaching approach, communication, and closure.

<figure class="feedback-quote">
  <blockquote><p>Nice teaching presence. Engaged with the class. Knew the content.</p></blockquote>
  <figcaption>Instructor, written feedback</figcaption>
</figure>

## Peer feedback

Peers who attended the lesson also gave written feedback. Their comments most often mentioned interaction with the class, confidence, and student involvement. Their suggestions focused on time management and pacing, more visually engaging slides, and moving around the classroom.

<figure class="feedback-quote">
  <blockquote><p>Strong connection with students.</p></blockquote>
  <figcaption>Peer, written feedback</figcaption>
</figure>

<figure class="feedback-quote">
  <blockquote><p>He is very engaging.</p></blockquote>
  <figcaption>Peer, written feedback</figcaption>
</figure>

## Reflection and next steps

The feedback affirmed the value of interaction in my teaching. It also pointed to pacing, visual design, and classroom movement as areas I can improve. For future short lessons, I want to prioritize fewer key ideas, manage time carefully, use visuals more deliberately, and move around the room more.
